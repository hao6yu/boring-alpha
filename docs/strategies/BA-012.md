## Mandate and fixed choices

Primary account: $5,000, fixed $1,000 drawdown tolerance, no deposits or resets.
Objective: beat a 6% annual cash scenario after costs; report 4% as context.
Neither rate is asserted to be a currently available guaranteed investment.

The risk target, shrinkage, buffers and screening thresholds are explicit
research policies, not values fitted for returns or derived from the user's
20% tolerance. An 8% forecast volatility does not guarantee a $1,000 maximum
loss. No alternate lookback, target, shrinkage or entry filter will be searched
to rescue this version.

The rationale is the published slow, diversified futures-trend evidence. This
extends the earlier BA-001/002 trend family; shorting, risk sizing and different
instruments do not erase its weak prior results. Volatility scaling may explain
some apparent trend performance, which motivates the control below.

## 1. Causal inputs and contract rolls

Use actual, identified parent contracts; never silently replace TN with ZN.
TN began trading in January 2016, limiting common history.
[CME launch record](https://www.cmegroup.com/media-room/press-releases/2016/1/15/cme_group_announcessuccessfullaunchofultra10-yearfutures.html)

The joint calendar contains dates all five markets are scheduled open throughout
09:59–10:06 Chicago time. Construct it before inspecting prices. Record
holidays and early closes. A reference bar must have positive volume and end
exactly at 10:00. Its last trade lies within the preceding minute: reference
prices are bounded-asynchronous trades, **not simultaneous midpoints**.
Prices and denominators must be positive. Missing expected bars remain missing;
do not forward-fill, delete the scheduled date, compress a gap into one daily
return, or choose another reference time retrospectively.

Use the nearest listed child maturity whose frozen exit date has not arrived.
The exit date is five joint sessions before the earlier of its exchange last
trading date or any known broker mandatory closeout date. At that exit, move
to the next listed eligible maturity. The parent panel uses the matching
parent maturity. Dates follow product-specific child rules, including MTN/MZC
expiry in the preceding month and M6E physical delivery. Resolve the gold
calendar discrepancy identified in the feasibility note before a gold roll.
Do not choose maturities using future volume, spreads or returns.

In prelaunch history, applying today's child calendar and multipliers to parent
prices is a **hypothetical exposure study**, not historical child execution.
Publish this label on every resulting table and chart.

For a non-roll reference interval, use one contract at both endpoints:

`r[i,t] = P[i,c,t] / P[i,c,t-1] - 1`

On a roll reference date, finish the old contract's interval, then set the new
contract's reference at its contemporaneous price for the following interval.
There is no return from subtracting the old contract price from the new one.
Account execution is a separate ledger: the old position earns its movement
until its 10:01 exit; the replacement starts at its own 10:03 fill. Mark and
report the intervening exposure. Never price a sequential roll using two
unordered opens from the same minute.

Build a compounded futures-return index without collateral interest or fees;
do not subtract cash interest again from this signal. Dollar movements use
the same within-contract differences multiplied by the **child** sensitivity:
$0.50/index point, $100/decimal bond point, €12,500, one ounce, or $5/quoted
corn cent. Parse Treasury fractions and corn units explicitly. A full window
requires 252 valid scheduled intervals and their endpoints, including both
contracts' references around each roll.

## 2. Direction, continuous target and integer selection

At the last joint session of each month, after 16:30 Chicago, fix
`s[i] = sign(product(1 + r[i,t]) - 1)` over the 252 intervals. Exactly zero
means flat. These directions first apply in the following joint session's
exit/entry batch. They remain fixed until the next monthly instruction.

Estimate demeaned sample covariance S of the 252 dollar-movement vectors,
using denominator 251. Set `Sigma = 252 × (0.5 S + 0.5 diag(S))` and
`v[i] = sqrt(Sigma[i,i])`. Update after each joint reference. Zero variance,
invalid prices or an incomplete window blocks risk additions; it is not
replaced with an invented estimate. If positions are held, commit their full
exit for 10:01 instead of silently reusing stale covariance. Missing held
valuation/fill information leaves the account unresolved and prevents a
performance verdict, even if a later exit can be recorded. An actual feed
outage does not authorize assuming a fill at an unavailable price.

Given positive equity E and nonzero directions, define:

`u[i] = s[i] / v[i]`

`tau = 0.08 × E`

`q_star = tau × u / sqrt(u' Sigma u)`

All-zero directions imply cash. This equalizes standalone dollar-volatility
exposure before portfolio scaling; it estimates no expected return.

Choose the globally best feasible signed integer vector q, including cash,
that minimizes `J(q) = (q-q_star)' Sigma (q-q_star) / tau²`.
Use deterministic enumeration or branch-and-bound with a verified optimum;
do not accept an unreported solver timeout as a solution. The stress budget
below bounds the search. Individual quantities may round upward only when
the **whole portfolio** meets every constraint; independently flooring each
target is not the algorithm.

An entry/addition portfolio must satisfy:

- Directions agree with the frozen signs and forecast volatility is at most tau.
- Either cash, or at least three markets across at least three asset groups.
- No market contributes more than 50% of `sum(abs(q[i]) × v[i])`.
- All loss-budget and funding conditions below.

Tie-break on J rounded to 12 decimals, then lower transition cost, lower
stipulated stress loss, and lexicographically smaller absolute quantities in
the fixed universe order. Cash is an ordinary candidate, not an error. No
separate turnover or hysteresis parameter is introduced; transition costs
enter feasibility and tie-breaking, not a fabricated expected-alpha term.

Between monthly instructions, hold if all constraints remain satisfied.
Otherwise search componentwise reductions only, preserving directions, and
require `sqrt(q' Sigma q) <= min(tau, sqrt(p' Sigma p))` using the same updated
Sigma on both sides. Removing a hedge can increase risk even below the cap.
Cash remains allowed. Rolls cannot increase quantities. Daily risk
updates can reduce risk but cannot reverse or reopen a sleeve until a monthly
instruction.

## 3. Equity, stress, margin and execution

Keep a marked cash-and-position ledger with commissions/data fees included.
E is equity after costs already incurred, before reserving future exit cost X;
do not subtract X twice. H starts at $5,000 and is the highest such equity at
daily joint references and execution/expense events. D = max(0,H-E). Do not reset H
at year boundaries. This protocol measures **daily/event-marked drawdown**;
it does not establish intraday maximum drawdown or an intraday stop guarantee.
Official settlements separately record variation-margin cash movements; those
cash movements are not additional P&L on top of mark-to-market P&L.

For absolute child quantities, stipulate:

`L(q) = 200|NES| + 200|MTN| + 250|M6E| + 200|1OZ| + 200|MZC|`

These are the feasibility worksheet's adverse joint price shocks, not
estimated volatility or a probabilistic loss bound. C(p,q) is the stressed
cost of actual transitions from holdings p; X(q) reserves the stressed cost
of closing every new holding. Count both contracts for rolls and both sides
of reversals. Per-unit stressed execution costs, in fixed order, are
**$1.11, $3.685, $2.90, $1.16, $5.76**.

Require:

`D + C(p,q) + L(q) + X(q) <= 1000`

`E - C(p,q) - L(q) - X(q) >= 2 × maintenance_margin(q) + 100`

For additions and replacement contracts, also require:

`E - C(p,q) - L(q) - X(q) >= 2 × initial_margin(q) + 100`

Use sign-specific September 10 broker requirements from the worksheet as a
**fixed contemporary margin stress proxy** in parent-history research, without
offsets. Do not call it reconstructed historical margin. Actual-child work
requires timestamped account/contract requirements known before the decision;
missing requirements block additions. Evaluate the actual transition sequence,
including any temporary concentration. Close old contracts before replacements;
do not assume offsetting margin across an unobserved two-leg order.

All candidate transitions pay their own costs. If existing losses/exit costs
already exceed the budget, flatten at the next executable opportunity and
record the breach; an infeasible optimization must never prevent liquidation.
A breach of the $1,000 drawdown budget or inability to meet maintenance
permanently halts this account. Ordinary risk-driven cash can resume only at
a later monthly instruction if feasible; no deposits or hidden restarts.

Monthly plans are computed after the preceding month's last reference, then
rechecked at 10:00 on execution day using completed information. Rechecks may
only reduce planned quantities. Commit reductions/exits by 10:00:59 and model
10:01 bar-open fills. At 10:02, after that bar and the exit records are known,
recheck the remaining additions/replacements. Commit by 10:02:59 and model
10:03 bar-open fills. Never use the later opening price to improve a previously
committed quantity. The account bears subsequent adverse gaps.

Within each phase submit in fixed universe order; fills may arrive in another
order. Exits finish before additions are submitted. Optional transitions must
pass the volatility, stress, drawdown and funding checks in every possible
partial-fill state within each phase, including incurred costs and remaining
exit reserve. Check these states before committing; terminal feasibility
alone is insufficient. Diversification and 50% concentration apply to the
**completed basket**, not its first fill. Otherwise entry from cash would be
impossible. Mandatory protective liquidation is never prevented by already
violated limits; record interim risk and any overshoot.

The batch completes by 10:04. If rejected/partial fills leave it violating
terminal constraints, commit a full unwind for the 10:05 bar open (or the
first genuinely executable native opportunity); include those extra costs.
Do not invent replacement trades in another market. A valid smaller completed
basket may remain. If entry size/pricing information at 10:02 is missing, do
not submit additions. No native fill is claimed from bar prices alone.

Parent exposure studies use parent bar prices translated through child
multipliers, plus one adverse child tick per execution in base and two in
stress, plus published fees. They assume hypothetical tradability; no native
child spread filter can be backfilled before launch. Missing committed fills
or unresolved held marks prevent a performance verdict; do not erase the trade.

In actual-child quote work, assess the entry spread/size before commitment.
Require spread at most two child ticks and displayed size sufficient for the
entire planned quantity; otherwise leave that proposed increase unfilled.
Do not redistribute rejected risk into other markets. Recheck hard risk/funding
constraints after partial execution using the batch rules above.
Protective reductions and compulsory exits are never blocked by the entry
spread ceiling. Their actual or observed friction can exceed the modeled stress.

Charge $4.65 on the first joint session of each calendar month, including
after a halt, for the three L1 feeds. Assume zero cash interest, no hosting
charge and no taxes in the initial study; disclose these limits. Actual

No monthly turnover count is imposed—the strategy generates its own costs.

## 5. Staged evaluation and stopping rules

**A — Sizing only, before return testing.** Use 2016–2017 for warm-up and the
monthly decision dates in 2018–2023. At each date use only preceding inputs,
fixed $5,000 equity, zero prior drawdown and no inherited position. Measure
feasible nonzero portfolios, integer tracking error, active markets, constraint
failures, risk and cash. Do not calculate subsequent strategy P&L yet.

A fast necessary-condition check can save work: shrinkage gives
`q' Sigma q >= 0.5 × sum(q[i]² × v[i]²)`.
If the minimum one-unit squared-volatility sum for any three distinct groups
exceeds `2 × (0.08 E)²`, no permitted nonzero portfolio exists. At $5,000 the
right side is **320,000 dollars squared per year**. This uses measured dollar
volatility when available, not the arbitrary stress shocks as a substitute.

If every decision is cash, reject the $5,000 version without changing its
parameters. Sparse activity is reported, not converted into an invented pass.
Repeat the same sizing-only exercise at virtual $25,000/$100,000 with explicit
20% simulated loss budgets and the same $100 operating buffer. These are
capital-sensitivity diagnostics, not authorization to invest more or automatic
backtests of a larger account.

**B — One development exposure study, only if A permits participation.**
Use 2018–2023, explicitly seen/context history. Run independent continuous
base/stress $5,000 ledgers, with no annual resets. Start in cash January 1,
using the preceding December monthly signal for the first eligible batch.
The predeclared final joint session closes all holdings at 10:01 with costs;
no new final-session entries. Calculate CAGR over the full calendar window
as `(ending equity / starting equity)^(365 / calendar days) - 1`.
Report CAGR versus the
compounded 6% cash scenario, marked drawdown, halts, margin headroom, annual
results, turnover, inactivity, tracking distortion and every expense.
Require resolved accounting/data, base and stress CAGR >6%, no account halt,
and marked drawdown <=$1,000 to proceed. Profit in every year is **not** a gate.
Parent-proxy passage establishes exposure economics under assumptions only.

Two fixed diagnostics: an all-long version with identical risk/account rules
to distinguish trend from risk premia; and a continuously divisible version
with identical limits but no integer/diversification granularity restriction
to quantify implementation loss. Fractional costs scale linearly only in that
explicitly unattainable counterfactual. Neither control is a second candidate
from which to select the best return.

**C — Reserved chronological confirmation.** Only after B passes, use unchanged
rules on 2024–2025 with a fresh $5,000 base/stress account and prior warm-up.
Document all prior economic/data exposure first: these years are not inherently
pristine just because a different instrument label is used. Existing MES
holdout files remain unopened in A/B. C repeats B's boundary liquidation,
cash, cost and risk gates. Do not splice its freshly funded equity path onto
B's path and present an uninterrupted 2018–2025 account.

Report dependence-aware uncertainty on net monthly excess returns over the
same months' cash returns, where cash earns `(1.06)^(calendar days / 365) - 1`.
Use 10,000 paired stationary circular bootstrap samples of n observed months.
For each sample, select the first index uniformly from 0..n-1. At each next
position, draw a uniform variate: restart at a new uniform index with
probability 1/L, otherwise advance the prior index by one modulo n.
Use NumPy Generator(PCG64(seed)); primary L=6, seed=12012. Separately reinitialize
for descriptive L=3/12 sensitivities with seeds 12013/12014. Record the exact
runtime/library versions. Use the 2.5th/97.5th percentiles of sample means with
linear quantile interpolation. Use identical sampled indices for trend/control
comparisons. A 95% interval
including zero is inconclusive evidence, not proof of no edge. Require a positive
lower bound for mean cash excess and for the paired trend-minus-all-long mean
before claiming statistical support for this trend implementation. Resampling
observed monthly returns does not reconstruct the adaptive account or establish
future drawdown probabilities. Do not require every annual return to be positive.

**D — Native execution evidence before any funded proposal.** Passing A–C still
requires the quote/roll audit, actual-child reconciliation, intraday drawdown
and cash-funding assessment, account eligibility, and consistent after-tax
cash comparison. Twenty quote/operational days cannot prove a slow strategy's
long-run profitability. No funded size, expected CAGR or loss guarantee follows
from a parent simulation.

Stop this candidate on economic failure; record inconclusive evidence separately.
No automatic timing change, higher risk target, broader universe or new strategy
follows. Corrections preserve invalidated outputs and seen-history disclosures.
Further analysis requires the exact input manifest and a provider cost quote;
no data charge is authorized by the protocol. The immediate next work is A and
the account-access check, not building a trading bot.

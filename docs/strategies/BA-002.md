## 1. The question

Can a simple blend of neighboring trend horizons reduce dependence on a
single lookback, while providing no worse drawdown and better after-tax return
than holding a lower-exposure allocation without a timing rule?

BA-001's 12-month rule looked better than its 9- and 15-month neighbors, and
its advantage over the exposure-matched allocation reversed in validation.
The [after-tax diagnostic](../notes/2026-09-04-BA-001-after-tax.md) did not
reverse that result. This proposal is deliberately informed by those findings.
It is not a new discovery, a fitted forecast, or a claim that averaging weak
rules creates an edge.

Published time-series momentum research provides motivation for investigating
own-return persistence across markets. Its futures/forwards evidence does not
establish this long/cash ETF ensemble's taxable performance. The particular
9/12/15 blend is our research choice, not a result established by that paper.
[Moskowitz, Ooi and Pedersen (2012)](https://www.aqr.com/Insights/Research/Journal-Article/Time-Series-Momentum).

## 2. One change to the trading rule

Keep the eight sleeves: SPY, IWM, EFA, EEM, IEF, TLT, GLD and DBC. Each retains
a maximum **target** allocation of 12.5%; actual weights can drift between
rebalances. At each final shared session of the calendar month, compute:

```text
H = {9, 12, 15} months
anchor(h) = final shared session of the calendar month h months earlier
vote(symbol, h) = 1 if TR_close[t] / TR_close[anchor(h)]
                       > cash_index[t] / cash_index[anchor(h)] else 0
target(symbol) = 0.125 * sum(vote(symbol, h) for h in H) / len(H)
cash_target = 1 - sum(target(symbol) for symbol in universe)
```

Thus a sleeve targets 0%, 4.1667%, 8.3333% or 12.5%. Equality votes for cash.
One horizon changing its vote changes that sleeve's target by 4.1667 percentage
points; simultaneous changes can still move it by the full 12.5 points.
These are correlated views, not three independent sources of evidence.

Use total-return prices, the existing cash-relative hurdle, monthly rebalance
and next shared session's open. Cash is not redistributed to active sleeves.
Use 10 bps per traded notional per side, fractional quantities, no leverage,
no shorts, no deposits or withdrawals, and a standalone $100,000 research
account per period. This is not a simulation of the eventual $2,000–$5,000
account's share rounding or broker restrictions.

Do not add volatility sizing, cluster caps, rebalance bands, alternate entry
dates, a zero-return hurdle, news or ML. An ensemble changes exposure steps;
it need not reduce executed turnover, taxes, drawdown or losses in a rebound.

Archive each horizon's anchor, asset return, cash return and vote alongside
the final target. A single return labeled as if it represented the ensemble
would not be sufficient decision evidence.

## 3. Comparison and tax assumptions

The primary benchmark targets **60% invested**, equal-weight across the same
eight ETFs (7.5% each), and 40% cash. Establish it on the first evaluation
session, then rebalance on each first shared January session using the prior
December month-end decision. Otherwise hold; realized exposure drifts.
This is not the usual 60/40 stock/bond portfolio.

The 60% target and roughly 20% drawdown objective carry forward the prior
design. They were informed by BA-001's already-observed development drawdown;
neither is an independently discovered optimal allocation or a loss guarantee.
Do not calibrate benchmark exposure to the new strategy's realized exposure.

Use the [existing stylized policy](../../configs/tax_policy.toml), subject to
confirmation before lock: ordinary 35%, long-term 20%, collectibles 28%, and
the declared base/low qualified fractions. Run all eight combinations of
FIFO/HIFO, DBC deferral/60–40 mark-to-market, and base/low qualified fractions.
Record the policy's full contents and fingerprint. These are scenario inputs,
not the holder's tax rates or verified fund-year tax character.

Return means post-liquidation after-tax CAGR under the named NAV-rescaling
convention. Drawdown remains pre-tax, net of modeled trading costs. The tax
grid does not bound omitted withdrawal mechanics, actual DBC K-1 allocations,
GLD expense sales, foreign credits, state/NIIT tax, or historical rate changes.
Passing this model cannot approve a funded taxable account.

Report cash, full static monthly allocation, ex-post exposure-matched monthly
allocation, and the original 12-month signal as non-gating diagnostics. Run
the 12-month reference with BA-002's common window and comparator; do not label
it a replacement for BA-001's archived result. Global-equity diagnostics remain
subject to the existing [source-or-unavailable decision](../decisions/2026-09-04-global-equity-benchmark.md).
No new proxy is selected here.

## 4. Fixed grid, not a parameter search

| Row | Horizons (equal votes) | Cost per side | Purpose |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Primary | 9, 12, 15 | 10 bps | The proposed rule |
| Double cost | 9, 12, 15 | 20 bps | Friction stress |
| Without 9 | 12, 15 | 10 bps | Dependence on the shorter vote |
| Without 12 | 9, 15 | 10 bps | Dependence on the old favored horizon |
| Without 15 | 9, 12 | 10 bps | Dependence on the longer vote |

Each pair divides its vote sum by two and retains the same 12.5% sleeve target
cap. Every row has its own benchmark at the same cost, on identical sessions,
and both accounts receive all eight tax scenarios. The 20-bps strategy must
not be compared with a 10-bps benchmark. Do not select a better row afterward.

All rows, benchmarks and reference rules use a **common 15-month warmup**.
Dropping the 15-month vote must not allow an earlier investment start. Require
the warmup and complete evaluation calendar before running; missing required
data is a refusal, not a shorter sample or an extra period in cash.

## 6. Selected return and drawdown bar

On 2026-09-04 the account holder delegated the numerical choice, then explicitly
confirmed the proposed numbers. The approved research bar
is a **0.50 percentage point annualized after-tax advantage for the primary
rule**, with maximum historical drawdown **at most 20% and no worse than the
benchmark**. Stress rows must retain a strictly positive return advantage.
This resolves the return/protection preference, not the remaining charter
review or authorization to run it.

For every grid row in each period separately, let S be the strategy and B its
cost-matched annual benchmark; D is positive maximum-drawdown magnitude:

```text
risk_ok   = D(S) <= 0.20 and D(S) <= D(B)
margin    = min(after_tax_CAGR(S, scenario) for all 8 scenarios)
            - max(after_tax_CAGR(B, scenario) for all 8 scenarios)
return_ok = margin >= 0.005 if primary_row else margin > 0
row_ok    = risk_ok and return_ok
```

This is stricter than requiring positive differences only within matched tax
scenarios. Report both, but gate on independent worst strategy versus best
benchmark as the earlier design proposed. Never apply a pre-tax Sharpe guard
as a substitute for the after-tax comparison.

The selected preference is **no return sacrifice for protection**. Require at
least 50 bps/year of advantage for the primary rule to justify investigating
its added complexity; require all four stress rows to retain an advantage,
without demanding the full 50 bps from each. The 50-bps hurdle is a judgment
call about research relevance, not an empirically optimal number or a
statistical-significance test. It replaces the draft's primary `margin > 0`
before any BA-002 historical results have been produced. The stress-row return
bar and all drawdown conditions are unchanged.

There is **no fixed absolute annual-return target**. If the benchmark's best
scenario returned 4.0% annualized in a period, the primary strategy's worst
scenario would need at least 4.5%; this is a hypothetical example, not a
forecast. Returns are nominal, compounded annualized figures after modeled
trading costs, stylized federal taxes and terminal liquidation, not inflation-
adjusted purchasing-power gains or a return required in every calendar year.

A 20% drawdown means a peak-to-trough decline such as $5,000 to $4,000. It is
an observed-history screen, not a stop-loss order or a limit that the strategy
can enforce in future markets. Past performance does not guarantee future
results, and market-risk returns cannot be guaranteed.
[SEC investor bulletin on performance claims](https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing/general-resources/news-alerts/alerts-bulletins/investor-bulletins-47).

Report the actual margin in bps/year. A 0.50-point advantage on $2,000–$5,000
is roughly $10–$25 of additional first-year return under simple scaling,
before omitted real-world costs. It can justify a research exercise; it does
not establish a meaningful income stream or compensate for arbitrary time
spent operating the strategy. Do not relax the bar after a near miss.

Before any economics verdict, refuse missing scenarios, failed tax identities,
failed account reconciliation, non-finite/undefined metrics, incompatible
policy/code/charter identities, incomplete sessions or an incomplete grid.
Invalid evidence is not an investment loss or an Inconclusive strategy result.

For the two seen windows, all five rows must pass separately in both windows
to report **eligible to request holdout evaluation**. Otherwise report
**not eligible**, with every failed gate. Do not pool windows to conceal a
failure and do not reveal the holdout to settle a near miss. Any behavioral
revision after these results is a separately logged candidate, even before a
holdout has been opened.

If separately authorized, evaluate the frozen full grid on the holdout and
report whether these same gates pass. Passing means support under the stated
model, not statistical proof or permission to trade. Failure cannot be fixed
by changing that test's rule or cutoffs. Report turnover, cost/tax drag,
exposure, worst month, sleeve/cluster attribution and the existing pre-tax
Sharpe-difference bootstrap alongside the gates. That interval does not test
the nonlinear after-tax CAGR margin or adjust away adaptive research.
Concentration remains a disclosed diagnostic, not a newly selected removal
test. No annual return target, weekly target or live stop-loss is implied.

## 7. Implementation boundary after approval

The [implementation plan](../superpowers/plans/2026-09-04-ba-002.md) expands
these steps into testable work packages. It does not lock this draft or
authorize historical execution.

Keep the existing engine and tax overlay. The bounded implementation is:

1. Add the equal-vote signal and per-horizon decision evidence. Pin all votes,
   exact ties, partial activation, common warmup and next-open timing with
   hand-computable synthetic cases; future price changes cannot alter an
   earlier decision. Put every behavior-setting input in strategy identity.
   Use an optional structured horizon field and compatible serialization;
   omit it for BA-001 so its existing decision records remain unchanged.
2. Add the BA-002 profile and exact grid. Make both `backtest` and `sweep`
   dispatch the selected rule; the former previously constructed BA-001 directly.
   Guard evidence runs against changed universe, benchmark, policy or costs.
3. Carry each variant's paired benchmark into tax scoring. Pass complete,
   verified tax evidence through final classification, not only pre-tax
   `variants`. Pin worst/best selection with intentionally different scenarios
   and ensure a missing/failing scenario cannot yield a favorable verdict.
   Add profile-specific research-eligibility labels: BA-002 must not emit
   BA-001's `Advance` label for a successful seen-history screen.
4. Enforce actual full-window coverage and maximum-grid warmup. Register the
   approved BA-002 dates separately; do not edit BA-001's boundaries. Carry
   seen/unopened evidence status through reports and approval gates, not only
   review filenames. Record charter identity in artifacts and classification.
   Pin an independent exchange-session calendar, including special closures;
   the observed price dates cannot validate themselves. A test removing one
   session from every price, cash and distribution series must fail. Compare
   boundary coverage with expected sessions, not just the last available row.
   Make the reveal record family-scoped across BA-001 and BA-002; a new
   strategy ID must not reset which holdout history has already been revealed.
5. Preserve BA-001 golden behavior/schema compatibility. Independently review
   the implementation and freeze it before any new historical sweep. Record
   every attempted sweep and failure, not only completed reports.

No new engine, fetched dataset, broker integration or model training is
required to reach that review. Reuse an identified archived snapshot only
after its coverage and methodology pass preflight; do not refresh history
silently. Synthetic tests come first, seen research runs reuse one confirmed
freeze, and first holdout access additionally requires an explicit reveal reason.
The profile-driven workflow uses one family journal and an explicit run context,
not separate per-run approval files or state attached to market observations.
Identical retries after crashes need no ledger edits. Reviewed code/evaluator
repairs on revealed coverage retain a diagnostic label, including their retries.

The [bounded NYSE calendar](../data/nyse-calendar.md) and its source record now
exist. The full dates and early closes match a version-pinned independent
calendar library; exceptional closures have primary-source support. A comparison
with SPY dates over seen history was subsequently reported by the branch review:
3,990 sessions, 2006-02-28 through 2021-12-31, exactly matching. No holdout
market file was checked, and no market prices were inspected to construct the
calendar. The
[workflow record](../changes/2026-09-04-ba-002-workflow-simplification.md)
and repository README describe preparation, confirmation and repair commands.

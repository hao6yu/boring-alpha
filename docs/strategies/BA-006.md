# BA-006 — a sized Coinbase sleeve: the only form the BA-005 bar permits

Status: **pre-registered 2026-09-08, before any sleeve weight, bill, or drawdown figure was computed**
Parent: [BA-005](BA-005.md), which failed both of its locked risk conditions while its terminal was 17x the index's and its break-even fee was
1,333 bps a side. This spec exists because that failure wrote a bar, and this is the shape that bar permits — not a second attempt at BA-005.

## Why this is not a re-scue

BA-005 failed holding **100%** of the account in one asset. Its step-4 statement is that a Coinbase-tradable candidate must hold the volatile
asset at a *fractional* sleeve and be graded against a blend, because a full-weight asset of this volatility cannot clear a 1.25x-of-QQQ
drawdown gate at any fee this venue charges. This spec does exactly that and changes nothing else: **same asset, same signal, same frequency,
same window, same gate.** What changes is the size, which is the one thing BA-005's own verdict said had to change, and which the objective
permits because it asks about *income*, and income is a function of both return and size.

If this fails, the Coinbase question is answered and no further candidate goes into this repository.

## The rule

At each month end on the archive's completed-month grid, the account holds a weight **w** in a BTC-USD sleeve and **1 − w** in **VOO (P0)**.
The sleeve follows BA-005's locked signal exactly — hold BTC above its own 200-day mean, hold T-bills below it — and the rest of the account
is VOO, untouched. The blend is rebalanced to target weight monthly, and the rebalance is charged: the sleeve's switches pay the venue's taker
fee on the traded notional, and the VOO leg pays nothing (commission-free at the broker, which is the conservative asymmetry, and no expense
ratio on top because `tr_close` is a NAV-based total-return series, already net).

- **Weights:** the fixed set **{5%, 10%, 20%}** — a factor-of-two ladder chosen before any number was seen, all three reported, no others ever
  added. The ceiling comes from the arithmetic of the gate: BTC's own worst fall inside this window was 76.7%, so a weight at which the asset
  alone could breach 1.25x QQQ's drawdown would be a spec written to fail; 20% is far inside that bound, and the floor exists because below it
  the seatbelt test (condition 4) cannot be evaluated against anything.
- **Window:** the archive's completed months from 2016-06-01 to the archive's last completed month — the same grid BA-005 was priced on.
- **Signals taken at month end, applied to the following month. No lookahead.**

## The four legs, every one reported at every weight

| leg | what it is |
| --- | --- |
| **signal blend** | w in the BA-005 sleeve, 1−w in VOO. The candidate. |
| **hold blend** | w in BTC bought and *held*, 1−w in VOO. The seatbelt test: if the signal cannot beat this, the model earns nothing. |
| **P0** | 100% VOO. The bar the objective is written against, priced before evidence. |
| **QQQ** | 100% QQQ. The archive's standing empirical bar, for the drawdown gate. |

## Metrics, on the archive's own conventions

`monthly_income_race.safe_amount` / `plan_stats` again — the functions that produced the equity book's $435/mo — at **5-year windows** (64 of
them at this record's length; one window is 1.6 points, which is printed with every failure rate, round 47 and r112), failure budget
**P(fail) ≤ 5%**, promise **"ends whole"**, plus **P(erase)** (the share of windows where the account is halved at least once) evaluated at
**P0's own affordable bill**, which is the promise the objective actually lives on.

## Pass — all five conditions, at the same weight, or it is a failure

1. **The affordable monthly bill exceeds P0's** at that weight, by at least 5% relative. Not the terminal: the bill. This is an income objective
   and a larger median terminal is not the deliverable.
2. **P(erase) at P0's bill is no worse than P0's** — 0% is what the index does on this record, and an income plan that halves itself one window
   in six is not an income plan (BA-005's lesson).
3. **Max drawdown within 1.25x QQQ's**, the same gate BA-005 carried, unchanged.
4. **The seatbelt test:** the signal blend's affordable bill exceeds the hold blend's at the same weight. A model that cannot beat owning the
   asset with no model is a seatbelt, and the objective does not need a seatbelt.
5. **A current Coinbase fee record exists** (`tools/venue_fees.py`). Costs cannot decide this candidate — the break-even is printed to prove it
   — but a *PASS* is a trade licence, and a trade licence does not issue against an unsourced cost (rule 103). A **FAIL may be published
   without one**, and will be.

## What a pass licenses, and what a failure ends

A pass licenses exactly one thing: a fifth forward book trading this rule at the **largest passing weight**, sealed by the same monthly loop,
with the same 24-entry bar before any claim of skill. It licenses no scaling beyond that weight, no second asset, no higher frequency, no
leverage.

A failure — condition 1, 2, 3, or 4 failing at all three weights — ends the Coinbase line. It is then stated once, plainly: on this record, a
Coinbase-executable account's best monthly-income configuration is the boring one, and no Coinbase rule earns a place in this repository. No
re-cut weights, no different signal, no longer window, no second asset. That sentence is written here, before the numbers, so that it is a
rule rather than a mood.

## Result, 2026-09-08 — priced the same day it was locked. The sleeve clears; the model does not earn it

`tools/ba006.py`, on 122 months and 63 overlapping 5-year windows (two non-overlapping looks at one decade), $100,000 paid in:

| weight | affordable bill | vs P0 | P(erase) at P0's bill | max DD | median | seatbelt | gates |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| — P0, 100% VOO | $831/mo | — | 0% | 23.9% | 1.24x | — | the bar |
| 5% | $919/mo | +10.7% | 0% | 24.1% | 5.47x | **−$23/mo** | fails 4 |
| 10% | $980/mo | +17.9% | 0% | 24.3% | 6.92x | **−$0/mo** | fails 4 |
| **20%** | **$1,045/mo** | **+25.8%** | **0%** | **24.8%** | 10.73x | **+$17/mo** | **clears 1-4** |

**Condition 5 is unmet, so this is not a pass: exit 3, `NO VERDICT`.** A weight cleared every gate that costs can move, and the spec's fifth
condition is a dated Coinbase fee record this lab does not have — the first time in this project where the missing fee is the *only* thing
between a candidate and a trade licence, which is exactly the condition the fee was made to police. Costs are not hiding anything: at the
20% weight the affordable bill falls from $1,045/mo at 0 bps to $963/mo at 240 bps per switch, still +15.9% over P0 at a fee four times what
any tier of this venue plausibly charges.

What the table actually establishes, in order of importance:

1. **A sized sleeve is the first thing in this repository to beat the index's income promise rather than merely out-earn it.** +25.8% monthly
   bill at P(erase) 0% and 0.9 points more drawdown. BA-005's terminal won by 17x and failed; this wins on the promise and clears.
2. **The model is not why.** Condition 4 — the seatbelt test — *fails* at 5% and 10%: the signal blend supports a smaller bill than simply
   buying and holding the same weight of bitcoin, and at 20% it beats plain holding by **$17/mo on an $831 base**, which this record cannot
   distinguish from nothing. The sleeve raises the bill. The signal does roughly nothing to it.
3. **The drawdown arithmetic worked as the gate predicted it would.** 20% of a 56.5%-drawdown sleeve adds 0.9 points to a 23.9% drawdown
   because the sleeve's worst months are not VOO's worst months. That diversification, not the timing, is the whole risk story.
4. **The sample is two looks at one decade** where the asset rose a hundredfold. Every rate above is one sample of that decade wearing 63
   overlapping windows as a costume.

## What this licenses, and what it would take

Nothing yet. If the fee record arrives and condition 5 is met at 20%, the licence is exactly one fifth forward book — and the spec now states,
before any forward evidence exists, that **the book must carry both legs**: the signal sleeve *and* the plain-hold sleeve at the same weight,
sealed as separate entries. The historical record cannot separate them (+$17/mo), the archive's standing conclusion says doing less usually
wins, and the only way to settle which of them deserves the money is to let both run forward and be sealed. A forward book that traded only
the signal would be choosing, on evidence that cannot distinguish the two, the more complicated of them.

If the fee arrives and 20% no longer clears at the real rate, this line closes as BA-005 did, with the same sentence: on this record a
Coinbase account's best monthly-income configuration is the boring one, and no Coinbase rule earns a place in this repository.

## Disclosure, added after the verdict and before any licence: the result lives in one start date

The gates above are unchanged — nothing here re-cuts the window, and a reader who wanted the window moved would have to write a new spec.
What was missing from the first print was the question a decision today actually has: *the locked window starts in 2016 because that is where
the archive begins, but no one can invest from 2016.* So `tools/ba006.py` now re-runs the entire test from start dates whole years later, and
prints all of them, unconditionally — no flag, because the disclosure that undermines the headline is the one that must not be switchable.

| start | months | P0 bill | 5% sleeve | 10% sleeve | 20% sleeve |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 2016-05-18 (locked) | 122 | $831 | $919 (+11%, fails seatbelt) | $980 (+18%, fails seatbelt) | **$1,045 (+26%, clears)** |
| 2018-05-18 | 98 | $872 | $931 (+7%, fails seatbelt) | $932 (+7%, fails seatbelt) | $925 (+6%, fails seatbelt) |
| 2020-05-18 | 74 | $899 | $902 (+0%, fails bill, seatbelt) | $902 (+0%, fails bill, seatbelt) | $893 (−1%, fails bill) |

The verdict line now says it in one sentence, and the sentence is the finding:

> a clearing weight appears at 2016-05-18 and nowhere at 2018-05-18, 2020-05-18; a decision made today sits closer to the later starts than
> the earlier ones.

Two things follow, and both are worse news than the first table looked.

1. **The sized sleeve's whole advantage is bitcoin's 2016-2021 run.** From a 2018 start the sleeve adds 6-7% to the bill and the *largest* weight is
   the worst of the three (a 20% sleeve buys a smaller affordable bill than a 5% one), and every weight there fails the seatbelt; from 2020
   it adds nothing at all and at 20% it goes backwards. On the two windows nearest to a
   decision that could actually be made now, there is no clearing weight at any rung of the ladder.
**The guarantee reading of this table is its worst rung, not its first.** `withdrawal_capacity.py` has graded every start since
round 4 because a monthly *promise* must survive the binding start, and the binding start here is 2020-05-18, where the sleeve adds
nothing to the bill at any weight. The +25.8% above is the best rung. Both numbers describe the same table.

2. **The seatbelt fails on every roll that has enough months in it**, which is the same finding as before with more support: the model does
   not beat holding the asset, at any start, at almost any weight.

What this does *not* do is change the locked conditions or the exit code. Condition 5 still withholds the PASS for want of a fee record, and
if a fee record arrives and 20% still clears at the real cost, the licence is still what the spec says it is — a fifth book carrying both
legs. What changes is the expectation attached to it, pre-registered here rather than negotiated later: **on the evidence of the two later
starts, the forward book should be expected to show no improvement over P0's affordable bill.** A first seal showing nothing is therefore
not a surprise to be explained away; it is the outcome the ladder predicts, and the book exists to find out which of the two readings — the
locked window or the later ones — was right about the future.

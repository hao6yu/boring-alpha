# BA-001 — Multi-Asset Trend

Status: **locked for initial implementation (revision 4)**
Locked: **2026-09-04**, before running BoringAlpha on historical market data
Revisions 2 to 4: **2026-09-04**, also before any historical market-data run

## Hypothesis

Medium-term total-return trends may persist long enough to trade. Assigning a
risky asset's fixed sleeve to cash when its trailing return does not exceed the
cash return may reduce severe drawdowns without destroying the portfolio's
long-run risk-adjusted return.

## Prior evidence and its discount

A twelve-month trend rule applied to a diversified set of asset-class ETFs is
a published strategy, not a new idea. Faber (2007) documented a ten-month
moving-average timing rule on a similar universe, and Moskowitz, Ooi, and
Pedersen (2012) documented twelve-month time-series momentum across asset
classes. Both papers, and the industry that followed them, examined much of
BA-001's development period. The development window is therefore out-of-sample
for this implementation only, not for the rule. Development and validation
results must be read with that discount, and the sealed period is the only
genuinely out-of-sample test.

References:

- Faber, M. T. (2007). A Quantitative Approach to Tactical Asset Allocation.
  *The Journal of Wealth Management*, 9(4), 69–79.
- Moskowitz, T. J., Ooi, Y. H., & Pedersen, L. H. (2012). Time Series
  Momentum. *Journal of Financial Economics*, 104(2), 228–250.

## Universe

| Symbol | Sleeve | Target weight when active |
|---|---|---:|
| SPY | US large-cap equity | 12.5% |
| IWM | US small-cap equity | 12.5% |
| EFA | Developed international equity | 12.5% |
| EEM | Emerging-market equity | 12.5% |
| IEF | Intermediate US Treasury | 12.5% |
| TLT | Long-duration US Treasury | 12.5% |
| GLD | Gold | 12.5% |
| DBC | Broad commodities | 12.5% |

The universe is fixed before the test. Sleeves may have different inception
dates. The portfolio begins at the first month-end at which all eight sleeves
have a complete lookback. For this universe the binding constraint is DBC,
which began trading on 2006-02-03, so the first eligible signal is the
February 2007 month-end. After that common start, a missing bar for any sleeve
is a data error that halts the run; it is never treated as cash. Capital
assigned to an inactive sleeve remains cash and is not redistributed to active
sleeves.

## Signal

At the close of the final trading session of each calendar month:

```text
anchor       = final shared trading session of the calendar month
               12 months before the signal month
asset_return = TR_close[t] / TR_close[anchor] - 1
cash_return  = cash_index[t] / cash_index[anchor] - 1

active = asset_return > cash_return
```

The comparison is month-end to month-end. If the anchor month has no session
shared by all eight sleeves, the month produces no signal and no trade, and
the absence is recorded as a warning in the run artifacts. Under the
ragged-start rule this can only happen before the portfolio's common start,
while it is still entirely in cash.

## Portfolio and execution

- Active sleeve: 12.5% target weight.
- Inactive sleeve: 0%; its capital remains cash.
- Signal timestamp: month-end close.
- Execution timestamp: next available shared session open.
- Direction: long or cash only.
- Leverage: none.
- Rebalancing: monthly, including active sleeves returning to their fixed
  target weights.
- Base modeled cost: 10 basis points per traded notional, per side.
- Cash earns the supplied one-session cash factor.

## Frictions

- Trading cost: the base cost above. The advancement criteria also require
  the result to survive 20 basis points per side.
- Taxes: the target paper and live account is a taxable brokerage account.
  BA-001's backtest evaluation is pre-tax. A lot-level after-tax evaluation is
  required before paper trading (see the review checklist) and is not an
  advancement criterion.
- Not modeled in this revision: spread widening in stressed months, market
  impact, partial fills, and whole-share rounding. Each is a candidate for a
  later variant or an execution-layer study.

## Benchmarks

1. Cash.
2. Static equal-weight allocation to the same eight sleeves, rebalanced on the
   same schedule with the same cost model. This is the benchmark used by the
   advancement criteria.
3. Exposure-matched static allocation: benchmark 2 scaled to the strategy's
   realized average gross exposure over the same period, with the remainder
   in cash. Reported as a diagnostic; not used by the advancement criteria.
4. Global equity buy-and-hold: **MSCI ACWI Net Total Return USD**,
   close-to-close, reported as a non-gating global-equity diagnostic. If a
   legally usable and reproducible daily series cannot be archived, report the
   benchmark as unavailable rather than substitute an ETF or synthetic proxy.

   Named as an index rather than a fund because no global-equity ETF spans the
   development period: VT lists in June 2008 and ACWI in March 2008, while the
   MSCI ACWI net-return series carries a December 2000 base date. Substituting
   a fund would silently shorten the comparison or change what is being
   compared, and a homemade blend would be a proxy chosen after seeing which
   proxies were available.

## Evaluation protocol

- Development: 2007-01-01 through 2017-12-31. Because of DBC's inception the
  first eligible signal is the February 2007 month-end, executed on the
  following session.
- Validation: 2018-01-01 through 2021-12-31.
- Sealed test: 2022-01-01 through the latest complete dataset.
- Each period is evaluated as a standalone run that starts from cash and
  executes the preceding month-end's signal on its first session, so every
  metric is computed within a single period. Each run's dataset must include
  the lookback history before the period start.
- Inspect performance across the global financial crisis, the pandemic shock,
  the subsequent recovery, and the equity/bond drawdown of the inflationary
  period.
- Evaluate the pre-registered 12-month rule first. The 9- and 15-month rules
  are stability checks, not candidates from which to select the best result.

### Period sealing

- Every run declares the evaluation period it targets. Data after that period's
  end is discarded before the engine sees it, so it cannot reach a result, a
  metric, or the data fingerprint.
- Validation runs are permitted only after a written development review exists
  under `docs/reviews/`.
- Sealed-test runs require both a development review and a validation review
  under `docs/reviews/`, plus an explicit unseal whose reason is recorded in the
  run's provenance record. Every sealed run is listed in this charter's change
  log. The sealed test cannot be repeated, so it carries the strictest
  precondition of the three periods.
- A run that violates these rules is not evidence about BA-001 and must be
  registered as such.

## Advancement criteria

All criteria use the base cost model unless stated, daily equity curves, the
metric definitions in the metrics module, and the static equal-weight
benchmark (benchmark 2). S denotes the strategy and B the static benchmark.
"Drawdown" means the magnitude of maximum drawdown. Each criterion must hold
in the development period and in the validation period separately.

- **C1, drawdown (primary).** Drawdown(S) ≤ 0.75 × Drawdown(B).
- **C2, risk-adjusted guard.** SharpeVsCash(S) ≥ SharpeVsCash(B) − 0.10.
- **C3, cost robustness.** C1 and C2 hold at 20 basis points per side.
- **C4, lookback stability.** C1 and C2 hold at 9-month and 15-month lookbacks.
- **C5, sleeve stability.** C1 and C2 hold when the largest-contributing sleeve
  is held permanently in cash, with all other weights unchanged. Contribution
  is a sleeve's share of the strategy's cumulative excess return over cash in
  that period.

Classification:

- **Advance** if C1 through C5 all hold in both periods.
- **Reject** if, in either period, Drawdown(S) ≥ Drawdown(B) or
  SharpeVsCash(S) < 0.
- **Inconclusive** otherwise.

Reported alongside the classification but not gating: the exposure-matched
comparison, one-way turnover, time in market, worst month, per-sleeve and
per-cluster attribution, and a block-bootstrap interval on the Sharpe
difference between S and B. Reproducibility is enforced by the artifact system
rather than judged.

A rejected strategy still counts as a successful test of the laboratory.

## Questions reserved for later variants

These are valid research questions, but none may change BA-001 after its results
are known:

- Does a 9- or 15-month lookback show a stable neighborhood around 12 months?
- Does volatility scaling improve risk without making turnover or parameter
  sensitivity worse?
- Does a short-term pullback entry improve implementation versus immediate
  next-session rebalancing?
- Should correlation clusters cap economically duplicated exposures?
- Does any public-information or sentiment feature add value after a price-only
  baseline and realistic timestamp cutoffs?
- How large is calendar luck? Run the identical rule with the decision date on
  other days of the month and report the spread of outcomes.
- Does an ensemble of lookbacks (for example 6, 9, and 12 months producing
  fractional weights) reduce the knife-edge behavior of a single binary rule?
- Do rebalance tolerance bands remove small trades without changing the
  economic exposure?
- Does tranching the rebalance across several sessions reduce timing luck?
- How sensitive are results to the fill assumption: next open, next close, or
  second open?

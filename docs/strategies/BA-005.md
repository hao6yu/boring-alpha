[`ba005.py`](../../tools/ba005.py)'s output; no Coinbase fee record existed, and none can move a gate that fails at a fee of zero)
Locked: **2026-09-08**, before any crypto return, drawdown, or bill figure was computed in this repository
Data frozen at: `data/crypto/current` → `snapshots/20260908T145853Z` (sha256 `1c6fb3d001d3…`, 7,831 candles, 271 requests)

## Why a new class, and why this spec before the data is touched

Every signal this lab has priced — trend, momentum, rotation, attention, news — lost to the index it was tested against, and the archive's
converging answer is that plain QQQ is the bar and doing less is the edge. The one honest excuse left is coverage: the panel has four equity
ETFs, some bills, and nothing else. Round 109 probed what is reachable without paying for it and found crypto dailies with a decade of
history, so the excuse expires today. A new asset class deserves the same scepticism as an old one, which means the rule and the failure
condition are written here, today, and not after the returns are visible.

## Hypothesis

Bitcoin's returns are weakly related to the equity trend signals already priced here, and its intraday 24/7 structure means a signal can be
held without the gap risk the equity book pays for. A single, unoptimised trend rule on BTC — cash when below its own 200-day mean, bills
when in cash — may therefore deliver either a higher terminal than QQQ on the same window, or the same monthly bill at a lower probability of
ruin, *after* the costs of actually trading it on the venue this repository can reach.

## The single rule, fixed now

* **Instrument**: BTC-USD, Coinbase spot, the only pair with a complete record here (ETH-USD is recorded and reported, and is not part of the
  test — two legs would be a portfolio decision, not a signal test).
* **Signal**: monthly, at the last calendar day of each month: hold BTC if its close exceeds the mean of the last 200 daily closes; otherwise
  hold bills at the same T-bill yield the equity book already uses from `data/current`.
* **No sweep.** One average length (200), one frequency (monthly), one asset. The width of a parameter grid is the number of chances to find
  noise, and round 57 measured that directly on the equity legs.
* **Window**: 2016-05-18 (ETH's first candle, and the first date the whole crypto panel is complete) to the archive's last session, which is
  **2026-09-04** as of locking. Chosen from the data's shape, before any return was computed. It may not be re-cut afterwards; a second
  window needs a new revision of this file with a reason.

## Costs — the part that decides it

* Assumed taker fee at locking: **60 bps per side** (Coinbase retail spot, the tier this repository would actually pay). This is an
  **assumption, stated so it can be falsified**: the run must re-price it against the venue's posted schedule on the day it runs, store the
  retrieved page's sha256 next to the result, and **refuse to run if it cannot**. A fee that cannot be looked up is not a fee.
* Spread and slippage: **5 bps**, stated as an assumption, to be replaced by a measured figure from a quote sample before any live claim.
* Turnover: reported, not hidden — the drag in dollars per month is printed next to the strategy's own number, the way the friction audit does
  it for the equity book.

## The bar, and what "fail" means

The objective names VOO and QQQ. Both are computed **on the same window**, on the same calendar, with the same costs applied to the
comparison where they apply to us.

1. **P0 is unchanged**: 100% VOO, priced before any evidence, and a bar moved after the results is not a bar.
2. **Pass requires** terminal wealth strictly above plain QQQ's on the locked window, **and** at QQQ's own affordable monthly bill (the
   5%-failure figure from the income work) a probability of ruin no higher than QQQ's, **and** max drawdown no worse than 1.25× QQQ's.
3. **Fail is decided by the ruin promise, not the terminal.** On the archived record the worst peak-to-trough fall is **83.8%**
   (2017-12-16 to 2018-12-15), the 2021-22 cycle alone cost **76.7%**, and the window this test will run over contains a **53.1%** fall —
   figures recomputed from the candles by `tests/test_fetch_crypto.py`, not remembered from a chart. A trend rule that survives those by
   holding bills is interesting, and one that survives by getting lucky in 2020 and 2024 is not. If (2) fails, BA-005 is **archived as a failure on this rule and this
   window**, whatever else looks attractive — Sharpe, Sortino, "it worked in 2024", or a different window.
4. **No re-scue by leverage, no re-scue by a second asset, no re-scue by frequency.** Those are new specs with their own locking dates.

## What a pass would and would not license

A pass licenses one thing: opening a fifth forward book that trades this rule on the first of each month, alongside the four that already
seal. It does not license a skill claim — that still needs the twenty-four monthly entries the forward test requires, and the objective's own
word for that is *months*, not *backtests*. A fail is a useful result too: it would be the third asset class in this repository where the
costs were priced before the story was believed.

## Result, 2026-09-08 — the same day it was locked, because one gate needed no fee

`tools/ba005.py` priced the locked rule on 123 completed months (2016-06-01 to 2026-08-03), on the archive's own month grid, at $100,000
paid in:

| leg | terminal | CAGR | max DD |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| BA-005, gross (fee = 0, not a possible world) | $12,215,315 | +60.39% | 56.5% |
| BA-005 at 60 bps per switch | $10,830,098 | +58.50% | 58.3% |
| plain QQQ, held | $697,992 | +21.05% | 32.6% |
| plain VOO, held (P0) | $428,014 | +15.37% | 23.9% |
| BTC-USD, held, no signal at all | $11,866,448 | +59.93% | 76.7% |

Three findings, in the order that matters:

1. **The drawdown gate fails, and the fee cannot rescue it.** The shallowest maximum drawdown the rule reaches anywhere on the fee grid is
   **56.5%**, against the **40.7%** this spec allows (1.25× QQQ's 32.6%). A fee only ever takes money out of a path, so a gate that fails at
   a fee of zero fails at every fee the venue could charge — which is why the runner prints FAIL rather than waiting for the fee record it
   cannot use.
2. **Costs are not the obstacle and never were.** The break-even taker fee is **1,333 bps per switch** to tie QQQ — thirteen percent of the
   sleeve per side. Coinbase's fee could quadruple and this rule would still be ahead on the terminal. That is the opposite of every equity
   signal ever priced in this repository, and it is not good news: it means the result is driven by an asset that rose roughly a hundredfold
   inside the only window its record allows, not by a model earning its keep.
3. **The signal is nearly free riding on the asset.** Timing added **+2.9%** over ten years against simply holding the coin — a rounding
   error on a decade that multiplied the money by a hundred — while cutting the drawdown by 20 points. As a *trading model* BA-005 is an
   asset bet with a brake on it.

## Step 3, same day — the ruin promise, priced with the archive's own definition of an affordable bill

`tools/ba005.py` calls `monthly_income_race.safe_amount` / `plan_stats`, the same functions that produced the equity book's $435/mo figure,
so these numbers are comparable to that one rather than a new invention with a similar name. The horizon has to drop to **5 years**: 123
months of crypto history contains four 10-year windows, and four windows is not a distribution — the archive's own round-47 lesson. That
leaves **64 windows**, which means one window is 1.6 points of failure rate and a 1.6-point gap is one plan, not a trend. Both promises are
priced, because a bill without its promise named is not a figure (round 54).

| leg, at QQQ's own affordable $1,019/mo | P(fail), ends whole | median terminal | P(erase, halved once) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| QQQ, held (the bar) | 5% | 1.38x | **0%** |
| **BA-005, fee = 0** | 6% | 4.10x | **16%** |
| BTC-USD, held, no signal | 14% | 6.32x | 32% |

The failure-rate pair (6% vs 5%) is the weak evidence — one window of 64 — and the tool says so inside its own verdict line rather than
quietly resting on it. The **P(erase)** pair is not weak: 16% of windows in which this plan sees its capital halved, against **0%** for the
index, at the same bill. QQQ's affordable bill at this horizon is $1,019/mo to end whole and $2,000/mo to never reach zero.

## Step 4 — the verdict, and what would have to be true for a Coinbase-tradable rule at all

**BA-005 FAILS both of its locked risk conditions** — drawdown 56.5% against 40.7% allowed, and ruin no better than the index's at the
index's own bill — while its terminal is 17x the index's and its break-even fee is 1,333 bps per side. It is archived as a failure. No fifth
forward book. The generalisation the objective asked for, stated plainly:

1. **Costs are not where a Coinbase rule can be beaten.** At monthly frequency a coin's own variance dwarfs any fee this venue could charge
   (break-even 1,333 bps/side vs a plausible 0–60). Anyone who argues a crypto result on fee grounds is arguing the wrong line — the spread is
   fractions of a basis point and the fee is a fraction of a percent. Any future candidate must therefore be *risk-specified* before it is
   return-specified, or it will be graded on the one axis that cannot lose.
2. **It must survive the drawdown gate, which rules out single-asset crypto at full weight.** BTC's own worst fall *inside this window* was
   76.7%. A 1.25×-of-QQQ budget is 40.7%, and no monthly timing rule on one asset of this volatility closed that gap — the brake bought 20
   points and still left 56.5. So a Coinbase candidate has to hold the volatile asset at a **fractional sleeve**, which converts the question
   from "beat QQQ" into "beat a blend of QQQ and the asset" — a question this repository already knows how to price (the sleeve and P0
   machinery), and has already answered in the archive's standing favour: doing less won.
3. **A timing overlay must earn its brake.** BA-005's signal added **+2.9% over ten years** to simply holding the coin. If the model's own
   contribution is under ~3% a decade, the objective is better served by owning the asset directly with a size limit and no model at all, and
   the model has not earned a monthly decision.
4. **And the P(erase) bar is the real one, not P(fail).** 0% for the index versus 16% here is the difference between a plan you can draw a
   monthly income from and a bet with a story attached. Any Coinbase candidate must be graded on it, at the index's own bill, on the same
   windows — a failure budget of 5% on 64 windows cannot even resolve a 1.6-point difference.

What would have to be true, in one sentence: a Coinbase-tradable rule has to be a *sized* position in a volatile asset — pre-registered
sleeve, pre-registered drawdown budget, P(erase) at the index's own bill — and it has to beat a blend, not an index, because that is the only
comparison that survives its own drawdown gate. This round does not add that candidate: stating the bar is the deliverable, and re-cutting
BA-005 into it after the fact would be exactly the re-scue the spec forbids.

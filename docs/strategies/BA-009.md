# BA-009 — U.S. crypto spot/futures relative-value feasibility

Status: **feasibility research opened 2026-09-09; no profitability claim, no
live-trading authorization, no final statistical test registered yet.**

## User mandate

Active quantitative trading, not ETF allocation or regular stock investing.
U.S. resident; Robinhood and Coinbase accounts; other U.S.-accessible venues
may be considered if there is a concrete advantage. Initial pilot budget
$5,000, approximate loss tolerance $1,000. Larger capital is conditional on
evidence and a separate decision about dollar risk. Objective: earn more than
the investable cash alternative after costs, with risk reported explicitly.
Public fee schedules and labeled sensitivity assumptions are authorized for
research; account-specific fees must not block feasibility work.

## Mechanism and origin

Buy spot and sell an equal underlying quantity of futures on the same asset.
Study whether funding and changes in the futures/spot basis compensate for
execution, fees, collateral, cash opportunity cost, and residual risks.
Positive carry compensates risks; it is not a risk-free payment.

Initial scope: BTC and ETH spot paired with Coinbase Financial Markets (FCM)
U.S. perpetual-style contracts. Coinbase's public listing uses `EXPIRING` even
for its five-year perpetual-style contracts; identify hourly funding from the
contract metadata, not from the `PERPETUAL` enum for another product family.
Contracts and rates were inspected during this conversation before this note;
those observations are exploratory and are not unseen evidence.

## Feasibility questions, before model fitting

1. Can public metadata, executable bid/ask depth, funding timestamps, contract
   multipliers, and overnight margin be archived without credentials?
2. Can integer contracts be hedged with spot within $5,000, retaining a cash
   reserve? Initial screen caps each leg at 50% of total equity (100% gross).
3. What constant funding rate would be needed over 7, 30, and 90 days to beat
   cash after entry **and exit** costs? Report the required rate alongside the
   observed instantaneous rate; never call an annualized funding snapshot an
   expected return. Holding periods are sensitivity scenarios, not selections
   based on the best result.
4. Does futures collateral remain adequate during a 50% rise in the underlying
   without relying on immediate transfer of spot gains? Report this as a
   scenario at the observed margin rate, not a liquidation guarantee.
5. What cannot be established from the public feed: actual account eligibility,
   fee tier, funding-history completeness and event marks, executable exit
   prices, future basis, borrow/financing, collateral interest, and taxes?

First model: a transparent accounting and cost screen. No price prediction,
leverage optimization, parameter scan, or claims of alpha. No transfer,
credential creation, account change, or order submission is part of this phase.

## Evidence and next gate

Archive raw public responses with receipt times, source URLs, and hashes.
Validate book timestamps and depth; missing data is unknown, not zero.
Separate notional yield from return on all $5,000 of capital. Treat unused
collateral yield as zero until supported. Cash comparisons use 4% and 6%
annual simple-rate scenarios initially, not a claim that either is a fixed
available rate. Report pre-tax results and the tax limitation.

A cost screen can reject an unattractive snapshot or show the hurdle for a
possible trade. It cannot pass a trading strategy. Only if repeated, archived
observations justify further work will we specify entry/exit rules, a funding
forecast, risk controls, and an independent forward evaluation protocol before
grading it. Monitoring is not automatically scheduled by this charter.

BA-007's historical record is retained. Repairs to its simulator are labeled
diagnostics and do not restore unseen status to its revealed carry period.

## Why investigate this next

The initial BA-009 scenarios were burdened by two spot trades. Test a pair of
U.S. futures on the same underlying instead: a dated BTC/ETH future against
its perpetual-style contract. A lower modeled commission burden is a reason
to test feasibility, not evidence of a profitable spread strategy. Actual
dated-contract fees, spreads, funding, and margin remain inputs to verify.

Public product metadata archived on September 9 shows the September BTC/ETH
dated contracts and their December 2030 perpetual-style counterparts with
`view_only`, `trading_disabled`, and `cancel_only` all false. October and
November dated contracts are marked `view_only: true`; exclude them from an
executable Coinbase candidate unless later verified otherwise. Public flags
do not establish approval on the user's account.

## Hypothesis and accounting

If a dated contract becomes sufficiently expensive relative to the perp,
buy the perp and sell the dated contract in equal underlying quantities.
The hypothesis is that the gap subsequently narrows enough to pay for the
long perp's funding, four transaction commissions, and execution costs.
The reverse direction has opposite spread and funding exposures and must
be recorded as a distinct candidate if explored.

For long perp / short dated, using actual fill prices and signed funding:

```text
P&L = quantity × [(dated_entry_bid - perp_entry_ask)
                 - (dated_exit_ask - perp_exit_bid)]
      - sum(quantity × funding_mark × realized_hourly_rate)
      - entry_and_exit_commissions
```

Bid/ask fills above already incorporate the quoted spread; do not subtract
it twice. Depth, slippage, legging, and partial fills still need modeling.
The gap need not converge to zero: the dated contract settles against an
index while the perp can retain its own basis. Close or explicitly model
both legs around expiry; never silently leave an outright position.

## Bounded first experiment

1. Acquire synchronized public bid/ask depth for the four observed tradable
   instruments. Check timestamps, multipliers, funding interval, expiry,
   and tradability. Report executable gaps and round-trip cost hurdles.
2. Establish whether overlapping historical price data and event-time
   funding marks exist for these exact U.S. products. Archive provenance,
   inspect gaps, and distinguish indicative candles from executable prices.
3. Evaluate the spread with integer contracts, conservative separate margin
   requirements (no assumed offset credit), and return on the full $5,000.
   Initially retain BA-009's 100% gross-notional cap and $1,000 cash reserve.
   Stress spread widening, changing margin, and one-leg execution failure.
4. Only if economics and data quality justify it, specify a small set of
   entry/exit rules, a funding forecast using past information only, and
   comparisons against an unconditional spread, cash, and shifted/random
   entry times with comparable exposure and turnover. Freeze the chosen
   candidate before independent forward evaluation. Do not fit dozens of
   thresholds until one backtest passes.

Stop this feasibility branch if fees/spreads overwhelm plausible gap changes,
if usable paired data cannot be established, or if sizing and margin conflict
with the pilot constraints. A $1,000 loss tolerance is not a guaranteed stop.

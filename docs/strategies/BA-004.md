# BA-004 — Passive Capture

Date: 2026-09-05. **Draft charter — proposed, not approved or locked.** No BA-004
implementation, real-account configuration or historical evaluation has been
performed as part of this draft. This document does not authorize a run, a
holdout reveal, funding or trading. BA-001, BA-002 and BA-003 retain their
existing records.

## 1. The question

**Does this account keep the return of the index it says it is buying, after
every cost and every human act?**

Every predecessor in this repository tried to *add* a number to an index return
by forecasting something. BA-004 tries to *not lose* a number that is already on
offer. It makes no forecast, holds no view about markets, and contains no
signal. Its hypothesis is about this institution's plumbing and this holder's
behaviour, both of which are observable and auditable rather than estimated.

The reversal from BA-001/002/003 is deliberate. Those three asked whether a
number could be added to a benchmark and answered, in every window tested, that
it could not. The same records show the benchmark itself beating the strategy,
which means the largest available improvement was never a better signal. It
was the gap between a fund's advertised return and the number that reaches the
owner's balance.

## 2. Prior evidence and its discount

| Claim | Source | Discount applied |
|---|---|---|
| 91.99% of US large-cap active funds trailed their index over the 20 years to end-2024, survivorship-corrected | [SPIVA via Ledgertouch](https://ledgertouch.com/blog/planning-and-costs/spiva-scorecard-active-fund-underperformance), [FYFA 22-year scoreboard](https://fyfa.ca/charts/active-vs-index) | Fund managers, not individuals; the point is only that outperformance is rare, not that BA-004 is easy |
| The most-active households underperformed the least-active by 7.1 points a year | [Barber & Odean, Trading Is Hazardous to Your Wealth](https://www.quantmemo.com/writing/paper-barber-odean-trading-hazardous) | 1991–1996 discount-broker sample; costs are lower today, but the ordering by turnover is the load-bearing finding |
| Average retail trails the index by roughly 3 points a year, attributed to behaviour | [Lambda Finance summary](https://www.lambdafin.com/articles/average-retail-investor-returns), [DALBAR via Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/wesmoss/2026-01-27/how-the-average-investors-returns-compare-to-the-market/) | Vendor-published and methodologically contested; used to rank failure modes, not to size a target |

**The discount in full.** None of these measure this account. They establish two
weaker, more useful things: the dominant cause of personal shortfalls is
behavioural and mechanical rather than informational, and every one of those
causes is measurable from the account's own records. BA-004's edge, if it has
one, is not that it knows something about returns. It is that its failure modes
are countable in advance.

## 3. Instrument and benchmark

The candidate holds **one** broad, liquid, total-market equity fund, chosen at
establishment from a declared candidate list and recorded by name, issuer,
domicile, benchmark index and published expense ratio. The instrument list and
the holder's tax jurisdiction are **§8 decisions, not this document's to make.**

Two reference series, deliberately separated:

- **Tier 1 — the index.** The fund's own stated benchmark index, gross, from its
  published total-return series. Everything between this and Tier 2 is what the
  wrapper costs and how the fund is run: expense ratio, securities lending
  retention, sampling error, fund-level transaction costs.
- **Tier 2 — the fund's net asset value total return**, as published by the
  issuer.

The account's realised value is compared against **Tier 2**, and the Tier 1 to
Tier 2 gap is reported separately. The reason is attribution, not politeness: a
shortfall caused by a 40-basis-point fee is fixed by choosing a different fund
and needs no further research, while a shortfall caused by a panic sale in March
is a different finding entirely and must not be allowed to hide inside a single
total.

## 4. Signal

**There is none, and that is the strategy.** No trend, no relative rank, no
valuation, no drawdown trigger, no macro overlay, no discretionary override.
No new data feed is fetched, so BA-004 consumes no observation from the
`BA-TREND` family holdout and makes no predictive claim that data snooping
could inflate. This is the first candidate in this repository whose acceptance
criterion can be evaluated on its own account, in the present tense, without
touching historical market data at all.

## 5. Portfolio and execution

- One fund. Contribution buys it. No rebalancing is required because there is
  one holding; there is nothing to rebalance against.
- Contributions are invested on a fixed declared day each month, regardless of
  price, spread, drawdown, news, or the holder's opinion that month.
- **No sells** except: a declared annual withdrawal up to a declared cap, or
  liquidation on terminal withdrawal from the strategy. A drawdown is not a
  reason to sell and is not a permitted reason.
- Orders are **limit orders at or near the prevailing mid**, never market
  orders, never in the first or last fifteen minutes of the session.
- Cash is held to a declared maximum, in a declared vehicle, and the maximum is
  a constraint rather than a comfort level.
- Contributions arrive on the declared day, not on the declared day *plus* the
  day the holder got round to it.

## 6. Leakage budget

Each channel gets an ex-ante annual budget in basis points, declared at
establishment and pinned by hash. Budgets are declared *before* measurement, so
that the measurement can be an accusation rather than an excuse.

| Channel | Budget (bps/year) | Measured as |
|---|---:|---|
| Fund expense ratio and fund-level tracking difference | Declared from the issuer's published figure at establishment | Tier 1 minus Tier 2 |
| Realised spread and market impact | 3 per traded notional | Realised price versus prevailing mid at order arrival |
| Uninvested cash drag | 10 | Average cash divided by average account value, annualised |
| Contribution-invest latency | 5 | Declared day versus day funds were actually deployed |
| Account, custody and platform fees | 0 | Sum of assessed fees over average value; a nonzero fee vehicle is ineligible |
| Behavioural deviation | 0 | Count of registered violations, §7 |

The headline gate is a **total shortfall against Tier 2 of at most 30 bps per
year**, money-weighted, on a rolling three-year window, with the decomposition
above printed alongside it. A shortfall is reported in basis points, never as a
capture *ratio*: a ratio is unstable or meaningless whenever the benchmark
return is near zero or negative, and a strategy that only measures well in
rising markets is measuring the wrong thing.

Basis points, not dollars, are the unit of the gate; dollars are the unit of the
decision. At a $5,000 account, 30 bps is $15 and is not worth an hour of work.
The budget exists because it is **scale-invariant**: the same 30 bps is the gate
when the account is $50,000, at which point the identical plumbing is worth
$150 a year and the identical audit is the same document. BA-004 is built to be
correct at current size and already paid for at larger size.

## 7. Violation register, and why it is a gate

The novel requirement, and the one that justifies reusing this repository's
freeze machinery: at establishment the holder signs a **closed list of
behaviours that count as violations**, reviewed and never expanded while a
period is open. Candidate entries, to be pruned to the real list at §8:

1. Looking at, or recomputing anything about, the account inside a declared
   blackout window, which includes at minimum the drawdown periods the charter
   declares in advance.
2. Any sell outside §5.
3. Any change to allocation, instrument, budget or schedule outside the
   declared annual review.
4. Holding cash above the declared maximum for any reason, including
   waiting for a better price.
5. Adding an instrument, however small or temporary.
6. Any new rule invented during the period, however defensible afterwards.

The count of violations is a **first-class acceptance criterion, reported with
the same authority as the return**, because the return is only interpretable
conditional on it. A 12-bps shortfall alongside one violation is not a pass: it
is a 12-bps shortfall from a run that was not the registered one, which is
precisely the failure mode that made BA-001, BA-002 and BA-003 worth so little.
Existing freeze, seal and confirmation machinery applies to the policy and the
register, with the holder rather than a dataset as the thing being constrained.
A violation is recorded by appending, never by editing, and a period with a
violation is reported as a violation.

## 8. Why this can be answered when the others could not

BA-001/002/003 estimated a *mean difference in returns* between two nearly
identical portfolios, where the difference series is event-dominated and the
effective number of independent decisions is on the order of fifteen. That is a
statistical inference, and its natural honest output is the word Inconclusive.

BA-004 estimates nothing. Its shortfall is an **accounting identity**: a sum of
measured terms, each independently observable, none requiring a distributional
assumption or a stationary regime. It can therefore be *decisively* assessed
after twelve months and definitively after thirty-six, and if it fails it names
the channel that failed. That is the property BA-003's screen lacked and the
reason to prefer this question to that one.

The minimum detectable effect follows from the same structure: with a declared
30-bps budget split across six channels, a single leaked month of excess cash or
one badly filled order is visible at once, because the terms are measured
individually rather than inferred from an annual aggregate.

## 9. Limitations, stated plainly

- **BA-004 cannot tell you whether you own the right thing.** It measures
  retention of whatever §3 selected. A capture strategy can pass every gate in
  this document and still hold a poor portfolio. Choice of beta is a separate
  decision from retention, and this charter deliberately refuses to make it,
  because a strategy that audits retention cannot also be arguing for the asset
  it retains.
- **The 20% drawdown ceiling that governed BA-001/002/003 does not appear here,
  and that is a substantive amendment, not an omission.** A never-de-risking
  equity book has equity drawdowns; the archived annual benchmark in seen A
  reached 20.50%, so that ceiling was always a demand to hold equities without
  experiencing them. BA-004 instead requires the holder to declare, in advance
  and in writing, the peak-to-trough decline they expect, will not act on, and
  will treat as the cost of the premium rather than as an error. The drawdown
  appears in this charter as a **budget on behaviour during drawdowns**, not as
  a budget on drawdowns themselves.
- Three years is the shortest window that separates a budget miss from a
  quarterly accident. Nothing here can be concluded from one year.
- Realised spread measured against the mid at arrival understates the cost of
  the decision to trade at all, and no unfunded cash-flow assumption is
  modelled: the schedule in §5 is a rule, not a forecast of the holder's
  savings.
- Money-weighted return, §10, is the correct metric for a contributing account
  and is not comparable to the time-weighted CAGRs reported for BA-001/002/003.
  Comparing them would be an error and the reporting layer must refuse it.

## 10. Implementation sequence

1. **Decide §8 and pin the identities.** No code before the instrument,
   jurisdiction, budgets, blackout windows and violation register are agreed,
   hashed and written down.
2. **External cash flows and money-weighted return.** The engine is
   self-financing today: `metrics/performance.py` computes
   `cagr = (end_equity / start_equity) ** (365.2425 / elapsed_days) - 1`, and the
   only existing notion of deposits is the tax overlay's proportional NAV
   rescaling, which states in its own convention string that it is not an exact
   deposit simulation and suppresses contribution-mode CAGR. So every figure in
   this repository describes a lump sum invested on day one and never
   contributed to again — which is not this account, and probably not the
   holder's next thirty years. This work delivers an explicit cash-flow stream,
   a money-weighted annualised return, and a Modified Dietz approximation as a
   cross-check. **It is also the correction that makes every future result in
   this repository decision-relevant, and it is BA-004's principal engineering
   deliverable.**
3. **The realised-cost ledger.** Ingest actual fills, record prevailing mid and
   timestamp at arrival, and compute the §6 decomposition against published
   Tier 1 and Tier 2 series. This is the layer `execution/simulator.py` says it
   is meant to be replaced by. The simulator's flat cost in basis points is
   currently an untested assumption; ten real prints will replace the guess with
   a measurement, and that measurement is the number that makes everything else
   in this repository worth more.
4. **Policy lock.** Reuse the freeze, seal and confirmation code with the
   *policy* as the sealed object: refuse to recompute or re-display anything
   during a declared blackout, append every decision and deviation, and on
   request replay the sealed policy back to the holder. The mechanism already
   exists and was built for datasets.
5. **First audit.** One year, one report, this document unchanged. Its verdict
   is a number and an integer violation count. Nothing else.

## 11. Stopping rule and what passing means

- A Tier 1 to Tier 2 shortfall above budget at the annual review is an
  instrument-selection failure. Fixing it means replacing the fund at the next
  annual review, which is a fee comparison rather than a strategy change and is
  permitted.
- A Tier 2 shortfall above budget with zero violations is a plumbing failure:
  fix the channel named, re-declare that channel's budget once, log the
  re-declaration, and do not touch anything else.
- One or more violations voids the period's return figure as evidence about the
  strategy, and is reported as the finding in its own right. The remedy is the
  policy lock, tightened, not a signal.
- **Passing means the holder reliably gets what they buy, at current size.**
  That is the whole claim. It does not authorise funding, leverage, a second
  instrument, or a signal, and it does not make BA-004 a trading strategy.
- Passing is **not** sufficient for a strategy to be worth funding. See §13.
- BA-004 makes no prediction and consumes no holdout observation, so it neither
  releases nor spends the family's sealed window. If a genuine predictive
  hypothesis ever warrants one, the option is still unspent.

## 12. Decisions required before pinning

Not yet approved, and none of them inferable from this document:

1. Instrument candidate list and the selected fund, with its published expense
   ratio pinned as a number.
2. ~~Tax jurisdiction~~ **resolved 2026-09-05: United States federal taxpayer.**
   The $60,000 non-resident-alien estate-tax trap therefore does not apply, and
   US-domiciled ETFs remain the right vehicle class. Resolution surfaced a
   question this charter did not previously ask: §6 has no budget line for the
   annual tax on dividends, which is the one tax term a taxable account cannot
   optimise away. At a broad-market yield near 1.2% that term is roughly 18 bps a
   year at a 15% qualified rate and roughly zero at the 0% long-term capital
   gains rate — i.e. between nothing and 60% of the entire §6 gate, depending on
   a fact about the holder's income rather than about markets. A tax-advantaged
   container is therefore a **§12 decision prior to instrument selection**, not a
   later refinement, because the container changes the gate before any budget is
   declared. The charter's tax layer remains deliberately absent rather than
   assumed zero: inside a retirement container the repository's lot, wash-sale and
   carryover machinery is inert, since a sale there has no capital consequence at
   all.
3. Container: retirement account versus taxable brokerage, and which kind.
4. Contribution schedule and amount — the term that actually dominates the
   dollar outcome at current account size, and the one no backtest in this
   repository has ever modelled.
5. The declared drawdown expectation and blackout windows (§9).
6. The final violation register (§7), pruned to what the holder will actually
   sign.
7. The §6 budgets, and whether 30 bps is the right headline for the chosen
   instrument.

## 13. Benchmark dominance (added 2026-09-05, after the first grid ran)

Written after results, and logged as such, so it does not retroactively change
what was pre-registered. It exists because the §6 gate has a hole the number
made obvious, and the hole is the reviewer's:

**A criterion measured against the strategy's own index cannot detect the one
failure mode that matters, which is that the strategy should not exist.**

BA-004's benchmark is the fund it holds. It has no signal (§4), so on any
benchmark that moves with that fund it is guaranteed to land within its fee.
Passing is close to a tautology. A strategy that cannot lose to its own index is
not a good strategy; it is a description of holding that index. The correct
reading of a clean BA-004 result is therefore not "it works" but "the plumbing
is sound and there is nothing here to fund".

So the repo gains a dominance rule, and it is now applied before the retention
gate, not after:

**P0 — Dominance.** Run the same contribution schedule into the plain index,
commission-free, no strategy, once per candidate period. A candidate whose
money-weighted return does not exceed that comparator *net of every cost the
holder would actually pay* is classified **Redundant**, and it does not matter
how well it tracks anything else. Redundant is a kill, and it is the kill that
applies to BA-004.

Two consequences worth writing down before anyone runs the numbers:

1. Redundant is not the same as bad. BA-004 passes the audit battery, and the
   audit harness is worth keeping for exactly the reason in §6 — the leakage it
   measures is real and it is the thing that quietly kills the other three
   strategies. What fails is the *decision* to call a tracking vehicle a
   strategy.
2. Instrument choice dominates strategy choice at this account size, by a wide
   margin, and the grid has no row for it. Between the funds already in the
   snapshot the spread is not tens of basis points, it is the difference between
   roughly $91k and $140k on the same schedule. Any future budget spent
   optimising execution order is being taken from a much larger line item.

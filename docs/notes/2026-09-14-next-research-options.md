# Options after the fixed-score follow-up

Reviewed September 14, 2026. This is an options review, not authorization for
another experiment, data purchase, recurring monitor or funded account.

The [latest follow-up](../../research/ml-stock-followup-2026-09-14/RESULTS.md)
supports retaining the fixed score as an exploratory lead. It still fails the
4–6% account-return target; its selection-advantage interval includes zero.
The trained tree and Ridge versions remain shelved.

## User follow-up: prefer recent history through 2026

The user asked why the evaluation cannot cover 2022 through current 2026.
The existing 2023 cutoff was a research choice: 2024–2025 were reserved for
later evaluation. It was not an established vendor access cutoff. Tiingo
documents historical date-range requests and current end-of-day updates;
complete coverage for our securities and their later corporate actions still
requires checking. [Tiingo documentation](https://www.tiingo.com/documentation/end-of-day).

The recommended next evaluation is therefore the unchanged fixed score on
2024 through the latest fully available 2026 trading session, with the
already-seen 2022–2023 results reported separately. Freeze the strategy and
account rules before inspecting the later outcomes; extend financial inputs
using their historical filing dates and qualify later corporate actions.
Report 2026 as year-to-date. This is historical validation, not forward paper
trading, and complete coverage is not yet verified. This options discussion
has not downloaded or evaluated those reserved prices.

## Lower-cost alternative: earlier cached fixed-score history

The existing monthly panel includes June 2019–December 2021, in addition to the
2022–2023 diagnostic. The fixed score does not require model fitting. These
earlier observations can support another chronological diagnostic of the
unchanged formula, eligible historical cohort and declared account rules.
The 2018 cohort must not be projected into years before it was selected.

Keep this explicitly exploratory: the earlier years were training data for
the learned models, and the fixed-score follow-up was chosen after seeing
2022–2023. They are not a new untouched holdout. Some earlier stock distributions
and merger accounting need extending even though their prices and contractual
entitlements are cached. Do not promise an exact whole-share account result
before checking those paths. Keep unavailable payments explicit.

Suggested next scope: one unchanged fixed-score comparison on the earlier
cached period, a bounded account-qualification check, the same control/cost
discipline and $0 new paid data. No feature/threshold search or reserved-year
release is implied. A broader historical study would require correctly dated
older cohorts, not simply more history for surviving 2018 names.

## Forward paper tracking

Freeze the current score and collect timestamped future signals and simulated
fills. This would assess current data availability and operation without risking
capital, but a few months cannot establish dependable annual returns. The live
feature path and any broker paper interface still need qualification. No
automation or paper-trading deployment has been created by this review.

## Distinct research lead: announced all-cash merger arbitrage

Study purchases after a definitive all-cash acquisition announcement, where
the offered consideration exceeds the target's market price. The spread
compensates for uncertain closing, delay and other risks. Failed deals can
cause substantial losses; this is not risk-free convergence. Research by
Heath and Mitchell examines how cancellation risk changes with market returns.
[Authors' research summary](https://www.aqr.com/Insights/Research/Working-Paper/Market-Crashes-and-Merger-Completions).

A July 2026 paper by Balyasny researchers studies language-model forecasts of
deal outcomes using expert-designed context and supervised fine-tuning. It
reports forecasting scores and decision-support deployment, not a verified
profitable $10,000 retail trading strategy. Ordinary frontier models did not
automatically outperform its structured baseline. Historical language-model
evaluation also requires protection against knowledge and document leakage.
[Paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.09921v1).

This family has not been backtested in the repository; recording merger
proceeds in other accounts was corporate-action accounting, not a merger-arb
strategy. It would first need a short evidence/data feasibility review covering
failed as well as completed deals, announcement-time terms, realistic entry
spreads, capital tied up, losses on cancellation and costs. Publicly available
deal records and the ability to outperform market-implied expectations are not
yet established for our implementation. No expected return is assigned.

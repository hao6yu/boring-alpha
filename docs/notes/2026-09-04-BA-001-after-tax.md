# BA-001 after tax: a post-hoc diagnostic

**This diagnostic was computed after BA-001's results were known. It does not
change BA-001's Inconclusive classification or supply preregistered evidence
for BA-002. The sealed period remains unrevealed.**

Under every tested tax scenario, the strategy outperformed the exposure-matched
allocation in development and underperformed it in validation. Accounting for
taxes does not reverse that change between periods.

## Comparison and sensitivity

Each interval below is the minimum to maximum difference across the eight
**matched** scenarios. A positive return difference favors the strategy.
A positive drag difference means the strategy loses more CAGR to the tax
convention than the comparison account does.

| Period | Comparison | Strategy minus comparison, after-tax CAGR (bps/year) | Strategy minus comparison, tax drag (bps/year) |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| Development | Exposure-matched | +79.57 to +85.72 | +26.30 to +32.45 |
| Validation | Exposure-matched | −175.09 to −170.81 | −49.84 to −45.57 |
| Development | Full static benchmark | −53.29 to −38.75 | −12.81 to +1.73 |
| Validation | Full static benchmark | −402.69 to −395.03 | −102.97 to −95.31 |

| Period | Strategy worst minus exposure-matched best (bps/year) | Strategy best minus exposure-matched worst (bps/year) | Strategy's best-to-worst spread (bps/year) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Development | +75.54 | +89.64 | 5.25 |
| Validation | −178.19 | −167.73 | 3.58 |

Even allowing independently selected scenarios does not change the direction:
development's worst strategy beats the best exposure-matched case by
75.54 bps/year; validation's best strategy trails the worst exposure-matched
case by 167.73 bps/year. Those are observations about these two samples under
the declared model, not estimates of a future trading edge.

The strategy's own best-to-worst spread is 5.25 bps/year in development and
3.58 in validation. To identify the sources, the following table changes only
one axis while holding the other two fixed:

| Change | Development strategy CAGR change (bps/year) | Validation strategy CAGR change (bps/year) |
|---|---:|---:|
| Base qualified fractions → low | −3.357 to −3.345 | −3.584 to −3.579 |
| HIFO → FIFO | −0.682 to −0.571 | −0.0043 to +0.0003 |
| Commodity deferral → 60/40 mark-to-market | −1.322 to −1.217 | 0.000 to 0.000 |

The qualified-fraction assumption contributes the largest strategy spread
among these axes. That narrow spread does not bound omitted tax mechanics or
data uncertainty. Tax drag is also not uniformly higher for the timing rule:
it is higher than exposure-matched in development and lower in validation,
where the strategy earned substantially less before tax. A smaller tax bill
does not establish a better investment result.

## What follows

BA-001 remains Inconclusive. Its development advantage over holding less did
not persist in validation, and the tax scenarios tested here do not explain
away that result. The variants are diagnostic rows, not a menu from which a
new winner was selected.

The next research step is a separate BA-002 hypothesis and charter stating
why a timing rule might earn its cost against the annual 60%-target benchmark.
That charter must disclose the development and validation results already
seen. The tax conventions and limitations in the
[correction record](../changes/2026-09-04-after-tax-corrections.md) remain part
of interpreting any later comparison. No sealed-period run, parameter search,
new market-data fetch or live trade was performed for this diagnostic.
